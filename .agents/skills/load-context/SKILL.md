## What it is

An embeddable donation / tipping widget that lives **directly inside the website owner's own page**, rather than sending supporters off to an external profile or hosted platform. The widget *is* the product — not a marketing surface for a hosted page. A supporter can give in a couple of taps without ever leaving the site.

## The core argument (lead with this everywhere)

Every redirect to a separate donation page loses people — the willing supporters fall away in the gap between "I want to give" and the checkout actually loading. Keeping payment **on-page** removes that gap, so far more visitors follow through. On-page conversion is the whole point of the product and the headline of every pitch.

## Who it's for

Open source project developers and non-technical website owners who monetise an audience — creators, writers, musicians, and similar. They own their own site and want support to happen *on it*. The product is positioned as **quiet infrastructure**: understated, well-crafted, trustworthy — never a charity metaphor, never coffee-cup / tip-jar cutesiness.

## What makes it different

- **On-page conversion** — no redirect, no drop-off. The main wedge.
- **Polite by design** — once someone has donated, the widget suppresses repeat prompts for a while (server-side, so it survives Safari's storage limits). It thanks supporters instead of nagging them.
- **Engineered like a real payments SDK** — fast, seamless, part of the site rather than a clunky bolt-on. Supports the payment methods people actually reach for (wallets, iDEAL, etc.), with checkout that never shifts or stalls. This craft is the hardest thing for incumbents to copy.
- **A goal / progress feature** — progress bars that reframe giving from "buy me a coffee" to "help cover a real, concrete cost," which also lifts the typical donation amount.

## Pricing

No monthly fee, no plans. A **small percentage of what's actually received** (with a per-transaction cap). It only costs the owner anything when it's earning them something — incentives aligned. Method-based cost differences are borne by the creator, never surcharged onto the supporter.

## Positioning vs. incumbents

Buy Me a Coffee and Ko-fi are hosted profile pages with a basic button attached; the button is an afterthought (no wallets, no events, no SDK). The best donation-widget engineering (Fundraise Up, Givebutter) is locked to US nonprofits. This product joins the two halves that have never been joined: **excellent embed engineering, available to anyone who owns a site.**

## EU dimension (secondary message)

The product is EU-domiciled and built on European rails and hosting (EU payments, EU data, no cookie banner). This is a real structural advantage, but in marketing it's **deliberately deprioritised** — the conversion argument leads, the EU story supports. There are open VAT/tax questions (gift vs. taxable supply) that need formal advice before launch.

## Naming

Quietjar.com

## Voice & tone

- **Quiet confidence, not hype.** No exclamation marks, no spectacle.
- **Benefit-led, plain language** for a non-technical audience — say what it does *for them*, not how it works underneath.
- **Short lines, lots of breathing room.** The copy should feel as clean and unfussy as the product.
- **Lead with conversion.** If anything has to be cut, keep the "no redirect = more supporters" message.

These are the product-specific rules. The general house style (rhythm, banned words, formatting, numbers, how to reference competitors) is in `context/writing.md`. Load it for any writing task.
