# Positioning

## Statement

For people who own their own website and want their audience to support them there, QuietJar is the support button that only asks when it matters. You set a goal for a real cost, the button shows up on your site, your supporters cover it in two taps, and the button goes quiet until you need it again. Unlike tip buttons that sit on every page forever, QuietJar never lets your ask turn into wallpaper.

Short forms for headers and meta descriptions:

- Ask when it matters. Then go quiet
- A support button that disappears once the goal is met
- Set a goal, get it covered, and the button steps aside until next month
- The quiet way to accept support on your own site

## Category name

Use **goal-based support button**. "Goal" is the differentiator and belongs in the first sentence a visitor reads. "On-page" is still true but no longer unique, since Ko-fi and Buy Me a Coffee both have on-page widgets.

Do not say "tip jar" or "creator platform." Both invite the coffee-cup association and a fee comparison with Ko-fi. Do not say "campaign" in site copy (brand.md vocabulary), except in the publishers line below where it describes what the owner runs.

## The three pillars

Every section, post, and social line maps to one of these. If it does not, cut it.

### 1. Goals that end

A button that is always there becomes wallpaper. A goal with a finish line gives people a reason to act now: the cost is real, the bar shows the gap, and the button disappears once the gap is closed. It comes back when the next month starts. Ko-fi and Buy Me a Coffee have goal bars, but their button never goes away.

Proof: TCG Pocket Collection Tracker set a €50 goal for September 2026 hosting and raised €97 in 12 hours. Its Buy Me a Coffee button averaged €30 a month. Copy line: "Your visitors only see the ask when it is real."

### 2. Checkout that belongs on your site

Two taps, Apple Pay, Google Pay, iDEAL, Bancontact, no layout shift, and a design that matches the host site. Proof: load time in milliseconds, script size in kilobytes, a live demo, a technical note. Frame this as quality. Never claim competitors redirect.

### 3. Polite to supporters

Once someone has given, the button steps back in their browser for a while. With goals that end, this is one story: quiet when funded, quiet after giving. Copy line: "Your best supporters get thanked, not pestered."

## Supporting messages (not pillars)

- Aligned pricing: no monthly fee, a small flat percentage. Only costs you when it earns you something
- EU-built: one line in the footer or FAQ, a dedicated trust page. Never the lead

## First sentence by persona

- Maintainers: "A support button for your docs site that only shows up when the server bill is due. One script tag." Path: `/developers`
- Independents: "Set a goal for what your site costs. Your readers cover it, and the button goes quiet." Path: homepage
- Publishers: "Turn a support button into a campaign. Set a goal, show progress, cover the real cost." Path: `/publishers`, later

## Objections

Write each answer in under 40 words. Acknowledge the competitor's strength, then ask the question that exposes the gap.

**Why not Ko-fi? It is 0%.**
Only if you pay for Gold. On the free plan, Ko-fi takes 5% on tips by default. Either way, Ko-fi's button asks every visitor on every page. What happens when it only asks while there is a real cost to cover?

**Why not Buy Me a Coffee?**
It has the friendliest brand in the space. Does a coffee cup fit your site?

**Why not embed Stripe myself?**
You can, and Stripe's embedded checkout is good. How many weekends for goals that hide the button when met, suppression, design matching, wallets, and iDEAL, and who maintains it after that?

**Why not GitHub Sponsors?**
It is the right default for maintainers. How many of your users ever open the repo, and how many have a GitHub account? Use both.

**What do you take?**
The exact percentage, on the page. "A small share" loses every developer who reads it.

**Is it safe to pay on your domain instead of a known platform?**
Payments run on the same regulated rails as any checkout. Supporters see Apple Pay, Google Pay, or their bank's iDEAL screen, not an unfamiliar card form.

**Will it slow my site?**
Give the measured number: script size and load time.

**When do I get paid?**
Whenever you like, from your dashboard, once your available balance reaches 50 EUR (or the local equivalent).

## Proof to gather before launch, ranked

1. TCG Pocket Collection Tracker: €97 in 12 hours on a €50 goal, against €30 a month from Buy Me a Coffee. Done. Add a second site
2. Load time and bundle size, measured on a real page
3. Two or three installs on visible sites we can link to
4. A quote from a maintainer about the install experience
5. Average amount and time to fund, with and without a goal

## One line for the team

Lead with goals that end, publish the price, show the proof, and make a developer trust it in ten seconds.
