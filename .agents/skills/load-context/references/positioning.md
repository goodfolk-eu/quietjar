# Positioning

## Statement

For people who own their own website and want their audience to support them there, QuietJar is the donation button that keeps supporters on your page. Unlike hosted tip platforms that send visitors off to someone else's site, QuietJar completes the payment on yours, in two taps, with the methods people already use. It asks once, says thank you, and then leaves your supporters alone.

Short forms for headers and meta descriptions:

- A donation button that lives on your site, so your supporters never leave it
- Accept support without sending people away
- The quiet way to accept support on your own site

## Category name

Use **on-page donation button** or **embedded support button**. "On-page" is the differentiator and belongs in the first sentence a visitor reads.

Do not say "tip jar" or "creator platform." Both invite the coffee-cup association and a fee comparison with Ko-fi.

## The three pillars

Every section, post, and social line maps to one of these. If it does not, cut it.

### 1. Your supporters stay on your page

The redirect is where willing supporters fall away. Proof needed: one completion-rate number from a pilot, however small. Until then, cite checkout-abandonment research with its source and say plainly that our own numbers are coming.

### 2. Polite by design

Once someone has given, the button steps back. Server-side, so it holds in Safari. This is the feature no competitor has and it deserves more weight than the current site gives it. Copy line: "Your best supporters get thanked, not pestered."

### 3. Built like a payments SDK, not a widget

Fast, no layout shift, matches the host design, wallets and local EU methods pre-wired. Proof: load time in milliseconds, script size in kilobytes, a live demo, a technical note.

## Supporting messages (not pillars)

- Goals and progress: "help cover a real cost." Lifts the average amount. A section, not a pillar
- Aligned pricing: no monthly fee, a small percentage with a cap. Only costs you when it earns you something
- EU-built: one line in the footer or FAQ, a dedicated trust page. Never the lead

## First sentence by persona

- Maintainers: "A support button for your docs site. One script tag. No layout shift." Path: `/developers`
- Independents: "Let your readers support you without leaving your page." Path: homepage
- Publishers: "Turn a support button into a campaign. Set a goal, show progress, cover the real cost." Path: `/publishers`, later

## Objections

Write each answer in under 40 words. Acknowledge the competitor's strength, then ask the question that exposes the gap.

**Why not Ko-fi? It is 0%.**
Ko-fi's 0% on tips is real. The question is how many of your visitors finish the payment after the redirect. A percentage of more can be more than zero percent of fewer.

**Why not Buy Me a Coffee?**
It has the friendliest brand in the space. Does a coffee cup fit your site, and does a flat 5% with no cap fit your larger supporters?

**Why not embed Stripe myself?**
You can, and Stripe's embedded checkout is good. How many weekends for suppression, goals, design matching, wallets, and iDEAL, and who maintains it after that?

**Why not GitHub Sponsors?**
It is the right default for maintainers. How many of your users ever open the repo, and how many have a GitHub account? Use both.

**What do you take?**
The exact percentage and the cap, on the page. "A small share" loses every developer who reads it.

**Is it safe to pay on your domain instead of a known platform?**
Payments run on the same regulated rails as any checkout. Supporters see Apple Pay, Google Pay, or their bank's iDEAL screen, not an unfamiliar card form.

**Will it slow my site?**
Give the measured number: script size and load time.

**When do I get paid?**
Needs a settled answer before launch. See `open-decisions.md`.

## Proof to gather before launch, ranked

1. One completion-rate comparison from a pilot site
2. Load time and bundle size, measured on a real page
3. Two or three installs on visible sites we can link to
4. A quote from a maintainer about the install experience
5. Average amount with and without a goal

## One line for the team

Keep the argument, publish the price, show the proof, and make a developer trust it in ten seconds.
