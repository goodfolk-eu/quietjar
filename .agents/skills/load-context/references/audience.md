# Audience

Three personas. All of them own their domain and treat it as home. All of them have been burned by a tool that made their site look worse. The wedge is the maintainer.

## Sourced facts about the open source segment

From Tidelift, 2024 state of the open source maintainer report (400 respondents):

- 60% of maintainers describe themselves as unpaid hobbyists
- 44% are unpaid but would appreciate getting paid; 16% are unpaid and do not want to be
- 25% report income from donation programs
- 5% receive direct payments or donations from individuals; 5% from companies other than their employer
- 48% feel underappreciated for thankless work

GitHub Octoverse 2025: 395 million public repositories, up 19% year over year.

## Persona A: the maintainer (wedge)

**Who**
Maintains open source with real users. Has a docs site or project page on Astro, Hugo, Docusaurus, VitePress, or hand-rolled. Day job. Already has a GitHub Sponsors button and a FUNDING.yml, maybe a Ko-fi link in the README.

**Wants**
Some money for the work without turning the project into a business. A support flow that does not embarrass them on a carefully built docs site. Something that installs in minutes and never needs attention again.

**Hates**
Layout shift, slow loads, third-party branding, redirecting users off the docs, anything that nags visitors.

**Uses today**
GitHub Sponsors (off-site, needs a GitHub account from the giver). A Ko-fi or Buy Me a Coffee badge. Most often: nothing.

**Why they switch**
The docs site gets far more traffic than the repo. A monthly goal for the server bill matches how developers think about money, and a button that disappears once the bill is covered never nags their users. They will read the code, and good engineering spreads by word of mouth in this group.

**Where**
Hacker News, Lobsters, dev.to, r/opensource, r/selfhosted, r/webdev. Mastodon (fosstodon.org, hachyderm.io), Bluesky dev circles. Discords for Astro, Hugo, Vite, Svelte. FOSDEM, Open Source Summit Europe (7 to 9 October 2026).

**Asks**
Why not GitHub Sponsors? Why not embed Stripe myself? What exactly do you take?

**Why they are the wedge**
Highest tolerance for a new tool if the engineering is good. Cheapest to reach. Their readers are other people who own sites. The goal feature needs no persuasion. Risk: most likely persona to say "I'll just use Stripe," so have that comparison ready on day one.

## Persona B: the independent (second wave)

**Who**
Writer, illustrator, musician, podcaster, or photographer with their own site on WordPress, Ghost, Squarespace, Webflow, Framer, or a static site someone built for them. Can paste a snippet, cannot debug one. Audience already supports them on Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Patreon, or a PayPal link.

**Wants**
More readers actually supporting them. A button that looks like part of the site. An ask their audience does not learn to ignore.

**Hates**
The coffee metaphor once they have outgrown it. A button that sits there for months and brings in a trickle. Fees they cannot predict.

**Why they switch**
Goals that end, shown with a number: the TCG Pocket case study, €97 in 12 hours against €30 a month on Buy Me a Coffee. Design matching. Apple Pay, iDEAL, Bancontact for their European readers.

**Where**
Ghost and WordPress communities, Substack alumni on owned domains, IndieWeb. Instagram and Threads for artists, Bluesky and Mastodon for writers. Creator-independence newsletters. Theme marketplaces.

**Asks**
Will it break my site? Is it safe for my readers to pay on my domain? When and how do I get paid?

**Reach them once you have** a second case study, a few visible installs, and a WordPress plugin or Ghost integration.

## Persona C: the small publisher (third wave)

**Who**
Niche news site, local blog, newsletter with a website, two-person podcast network. Custom domain, mailing list, maybe a freelance developer on call. Thinks in reader revenue, has tried paywalls or memberships and found them heavy.

**Wants**
A low-friction support option that does not compete with subscriptions. Goal-based campaigns. Something that fits editorial design.

**Uses today**
Ghost memberships, Steady, Patreon, Substack pledges, nonprofit-oriented WordPress donation plugins.

**Why they switch**
The goal and progress feature. No cookie banner and EU data residency, because they deal with GDPR daily. They can activate a real audience quickly, so they make good early proof points.

**Where**
Ghost forum, journalism networks, The Fix, Splice, LinkedIn, newsletter-operator communities.

**Asks**
Does it conflict with our membership? Can we run campaigns? What about VAT on donations?

**Do not approach before** the VAT question is settled.

## The one message that works for all three

It only asks when there is a real cost to cover, and goes quiet once it is covered. The button looks like you built it. It says thank you, then steps back.
