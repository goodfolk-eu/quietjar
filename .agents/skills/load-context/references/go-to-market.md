# Go-to-market and content

Launch to maintainers first. Earn visible installs and more case studies like TCG Pocket Collection Tracker (€97 in 12 hours on a €50 goal). Use that proof to reach creators. The website is the centre and everything else points back to it.

## Site architecture at launch

- **Homepage.** The argument (goals that end), a live demo, the TCG Pocket case study, the three pillars, pricing with real numbers, FAQ.
- **Developers.** Snippet, install steps, performance numbers, how "steps back" works, methods supported, a technical note on zero layout shift. The wedge page. In the main nav.
- **Pricing.** Exact percentage, a worked example on €5 and €50, one line on method differences.
- **Compare.** Four short sections: Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Stripe direct, GitHub Sponsors. Fair, factual, strength first, then the question.
- **Goals.** The lead feature on the homepage and first on the features page: a goal appears, fills, and the button steps aside until next month.
- **Writing.** Not "blog." Text-led, in voice.
- **Legal and trust.** Privacy, terms, data residency, no-cookie statement. Boring on purpose.

Later: Publishers, WordPress, a showcase of live installs.

## Content pillars

Every piece maps to one. If it does not, do not publish.

1. **Goals that end.** Why an always-on button turns into wallpaper and a goal with a finish line gets covered. The TCG Pocket case study first, then one case study per early install. Invest here most before launch.
2. **Craft notes.** How it is built and why the details matter. Written by whoever built it. Zero layout shift, how stepping back works in the browser (and what Safari does to it), one button for iDEAL, Bancontact and Apple Pay, what we measure. Marketing that does not look like marketing.
3. **Owning your audience.** Plain guides for creators and publishers. Adding a support button to Ghost, WordPress, Astro, Hugo. Tips versus memberships. Setting a monthly goal for your hosting bill. The SEO engine for the second wave.
4. **Quiet by design.** Rare, short, strongly worded pieces on respecting the people who pay. "Ask once." "The thank-you is the product." "Why we don't surcharge your supporters."

## Channels, ranked

1. **Open source communities.** Hacker News (Show HN), Lobsters, dev.to, r/opensource, r/webdev, Astro and Hugo Discords, Mastodon dev instances. Post craft notes, not announcements.
2. **Direct outreach.** A hand-picked list of 50 projects with docs sites and audiences. Personal emails, early install, feedback. Target ten visible installs before public launch.
3. **Search.** Compare page and platform guides. Queries: "buy me a coffee alternative," "ko-fi alternative," "donation button for website," "add donate button to Ghost," "embed tip button."
4. **The "powered by" link.** Every install carries one. The real long-term channel. Tasteful, on by default.
5. **Integrations and directories.** Astro integrations, Hugo modules, Ghost integrations page, WordPress plugin directory when the plugin exists, Framer and Webflow later.
6. **Social.** LinkedIn for founders and publishers, X and Bluesky for developers and creators, Mastodon for OSS. Amplification only.
7. **Newsletters and podcasts.** Creator-independence and indie-web newsletters, developer podcasts. Pitch the goals-that-end angle and the case study, not the product.

## Launch phases

**Phase 0, before the site goes live.** Settle VAT. Install on two or three friendly sites and measure. Write the developer page, compare page, two craft notes.

**Phase 1, quiet launch, weeks 1 to 4.** Site live. Outreach to the 50-project list. Two craft notes published. No announcements.

**Phase 2, developer launch, weeks 5 to 8.** Show HN with a craft note as the hook. Lobsters, dev.to, Discords. The TCG Pocket case study as a long read. Small showcase of live installs on the site.

**Phase 3, creator launch, weeks 9 to 16.** Platform guides for Ghost, WordPress, Astro, Hugo. Compare page pushed through search and creator newsletters. First creator case study.

**Phase 4, publishers, after month 4, only with VAT settled.** Publishers page led by goals. Outreach to small newsrooms and newsletter operators.

## First 90 days of content

One substantial piece a week, alternating developer and creator audiences. Social posts pull one line from each piece; no separate social content.

**Month 1.** Developer page. Compare page. Craft note: zero layout shift. Goals that end: the TCG Pocket case study. Quiet by design: "Ask once."

**Month 2.** Goals: why an always-on button turns into wallpaper. Guides: Astro, Hugo. Case study: first maintainer install. Craft note: payment methods in one button.

**Month 3.** Guides: Ghost, WordPress. Goals: numbers from the first installs. Case study: first creator install. Quiet by design: "The thank-you is the product."

## Five metrics

- Active installs
- Time to fund a goal, and amount raised per goal against the site's previous monthly average
- Completion rate on the button, per site
- Traffic to the developer and compare pages
- Referrals from "powered by" links

Skip social followers, blog pageviews, and newsletter subscribers until the five above move.

## Do not

- Run paid ads before we have three case studies
- Launch on Product Hunt first. Wrong audience, wrong tone
- Write "10 best donation platforms" listicles
- Post product updates as content. Ship them, then write about the idea behind them
- Approach publishers before the VAT answer is in hand
