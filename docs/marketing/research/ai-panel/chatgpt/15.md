# 15: How can my open source project pay for its hosting costs?

Your open-source project can pay for its hosting in several ways without having to put the software behind a paywall.

The best approach is usually to keep the project free and open source, while making it easy for people and organizations that depend on it to support its infrastructure.

Here are the most practical options, from simplest to more sustainable.

## 1. Donations and recurring sponsorships

GitHub Sponsors

Let users sponsor your project directly through GitHub, including monthly contributions. Good if your project already has a GitHub community.

GitHub Sponsors

Open Collective

Particularly good for funding shared infrastructure. You can publish hosting expenses and let contributors fund specific costs transparently.

Open Collective

Ko-fi

Simple one-time donations and recurring memberships. Easy to set up and share.

Ko-fi

Patreon

Useful if you have an engaged community willing to contribute monthly in exchange for updates, community access, or other perks.

Patreon

## 2. Corporate sponsorships

Companies often use open-source software commercially and may be willing to cover its infrastructure costs.

You could offer:

Logo placement on your project website.

Recognition in your README.

A sponsor section in documentation.

A monthly infrastructure sponsorship.

An annual hosting sponsorship.

## 3. Sell a hosted version (SaaS)

This is often the most sustainable model.

Keep the source code free, but offer a hosted service that removes the operational burden.

For example:

Self-hosting: free, open source.

Hosted version: €5–€20/month.

Business plan: €50–€200/month.

Enterprise: custom pricing.

Users pay for convenience, reliability, backups, maintenance, and support rather than access to the code.

This works particularly well for projects that require servers, databases, background workers, or persistent storage.

## 4. Apply for open-source funding and infrastructure credits

There are organizations that support open-source projects through grants, services, or free infrastructure.

Some worth investigating:

GitHub for Startups / GitHub programs: GitHub .

Cloudflare: Cloudflare startup programs .

AWS: AWS open-source programs .

Google for Startups: Google programs .

NLnet Foundation: NLnet grants — particularly interesting for independent open-source projects in Europe.

Note that some programs target startups rather than purely volunteer open-source projects, so check their eligibility requirements.

## 5. Reduce hosting costs instead of funding everything

Sometimes the easiest way to make a project sustainable is to reduce its monthly infrastructure bill.

Depending on your architecture, consider:

Cloudflare – CDN, DNS, caching and serverless options.

Hetzner – inexpensive VPS and dedicated servers.

Fly.io – application hosting.

Vercel – frontend hosting.

Oracle Cloud – cloud infrastructure and free-tier options.

Also consider asking infrastructure providers for open-source credits or sponsorship.

## 6. Make the funding request concrete

People are much more likely to contribute when they understand exactly what their money pays for.

For example, publish a small budget:

Example monthly infrastructure budget

# €100/month

Application server

€45

Database and storage

€25

Backups and monitoring

€15

Domains and other services

€15

Example funding goal: 10 supporters contributing €10/month.

You can then put a message like this in your README:

Writing

### Support this project ❤️

This project is free and open source, but running and maintaining its infrastructure has ongoing costs.

Your contributions help cover:

- Server and database hosting

- Backups and storage

- Monitoring and uptime

- Ongoing maintenance and improvements

If you rely on this project, please consider sponsoring its infrastructure.

Every contribution helps keep the project sustainable and available to everyone.

Become a sponsor
