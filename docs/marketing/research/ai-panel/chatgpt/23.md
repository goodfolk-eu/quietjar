# 23: How do I ask my readers for support without annoying them?

The key is to make asking for support feel like an invitation, not an obligation.

Readers are usually happy to support someone whose work they enjoy. What annoys them is feeling pressured, repeatedly guilt-tripped, or made to feel that free content is no longer appreciated.

Here are some ways to ask without putting them off.

## 1. Lead with gratitude

Before asking for anything, acknowledge the people who already read and enjoy your work.

For example:

"Thank you for being here and taking the time to read my work. Your encouragement and support mean more than you know."

This makes readers feel appreciated rather than targeted.

## 2. Explain why support matters

Give readers a genuine reason to understand what their contribution helps you achieve.

For example:

"Creating and sharing this work takes time and dedication. If you'd like to help me keep doing it, your support would make a real difference."

Be honest and specific. People appreciate knowing where their money or support goes.

## 3. Make it optional

Avoid language that makes people feel guilty for not contributing.

Instead of:

"If you value my work, please support me."

"I can't keep doing this without your help."

"Please don't just read for free."

Try:

"If you're in a position to support my work, I'd be very grateful."

"There's absolutely no obligation, but your support helps me continue."

"Reading and sharing my work is already a wonderful way to support me."

## 4. Keep your requests occasional

A good approach is to:

Focus primarily on providing valuable content.

Include a short support reminder at the end of selected posts.

Avoid asking for contributions in every email or article.

Thank supporters without making non-supporters feel excluded.

The content should always remain the main attraction.

## 5. Give readers different ways to help

Not everyone can contribute financially. Offer alternatives:

Share an article with someone who might enjoy it.

Leave a comment or recommendation.

Subscribe to your newsletter.

Support your work financially if they can.

This makes supporting you feel accessible to everyone.

## 6. Ready-to-use example

Here's a gentle message you could put at the end of an article, blog post, or newsletter.

Writing

A little note about supporting my work

If you've enjoyed reading my work, thank you for being here. Your time, interest, and encouragement mean a great deal to me.

Creating content takes time and dedication, and if you'd like to help me continue doing what I love, I'd be incredibly grateful for your support.

There's absolutely no pressure. Reading, sharing, and recommending my work are wonderful ways to support me too.

Thank you for being part of this community. It means more than you know.

## My biggest recommendation

Think of the request as a gentle reminder rather than a sales pitch.

A useful principle is:

Give generously. Ask occasionally. Thank sincerely. Never make readers feel guilty.

That combination builds trust and makes people much more comfortable supporting you over the long term.

One thing I'd love to know: are you asking readers for financial support (such as donations, Patreon, or Buy Me a Coffee), or are you hoping for support in the form of subscriptions, shares, and engagement? The wording can be quite different.
