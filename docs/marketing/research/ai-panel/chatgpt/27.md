# 27: How can a musician accept tips on their own website?

A musician can accept tips directly on their own website by adding a “Buy me a coffee,” “Leave a tip,” or “Support my music” button that lets visitors send money using a credit card, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, or other payment methods.

There are several ways to do this, depending on whether the musician wants something simple or a fully integrated payment system.

## 1. Best options for accepting tips

1. Stripe

Best for a professional musician who wants tips directly on their own website.

Add a payment button or custom tip form.

Let visitors choose their own amount.

Accept cards and supported digital wallets.

One-time or recurring support.

Website: Stripe

2. PayPal

Best for simplicity and familiarity.

Create a donation or payment button.

Embed it on your website.

Supporters can pay through PayPal and, where available, cards.

Website: PayPal

3. Ko-fi

Best for musicians and independent artists.

Add a tip button or widget to your website.

Supporters can choose how much to give.

One-time tips and memberships.

Minimal technical setup.

Website: Ko-fi

4. Buy Me a Coffee

Best for a very simple tipping experience.

Add a support button to your website.

Offer preset or custom tip amounts.

No complicated website integration.

Website: Buy Me a Coffee

## 2. How to set it up with Stripe

For someone who already has a website, this is probably the best starting point.

Create a Stripe account.

Go to Payment Links.

Create a new payment link.

Select Customers choose what to pay.

Set a minimum amount if desired, or allow supporters to choose freely.

Customize the page with the musician's name, logo and colors.

Copy the payment link.

Add it to the website as a button.

Stripe supports this without having to build a payment system or write code.

## 5. A few things worth considering

One-time tips: Most important for casual listeners.

Recurring support: Let fans contribute €3, €5 or €10 monthly.

Anonymous tipping: Allow people to support without displaying their names publicly.

Messages: Let supporters leave a personal message.

Mobile payments: Make sure the checkout works well on phones.

Tax and administration: Tips received by a professional musician may count as taxable income. Check the applicable rules in their country.

## My recommendation

I'd choose Stripe Payment Links for a musician who wants to keep everything connected to their own website, with no intermediary creator platform.

I'd choose Ko-fi if simplicity and getting started quickly are more important than having complete control over the payment experience.
