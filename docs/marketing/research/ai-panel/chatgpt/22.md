# 22: Why does nobody click the donate button on my website?

If nobody is clicking your donate button, the problem might not be that people don't want to support you. It might be that your website isn't giving visitors a strong enough reason, enough trust, or a clear enough opportunity to donate.

This is a surprisingly common problem. Let's look at the most likely causes.

## 7 reasons people aren't clicking your donate button

### 1. Your donation button isn't noticeable enough

Visitors might simply not see it.

Is it a contrasting color?

Is it visible without scrolling?

Does it stand out from other buttons?

Is it surrounded by too many distractions?

Try: Use a prominent button with a clear label, such as "Donate Now" or "Support Our Mission".

### 2. People don't understand why they should donate

This is probably one of the biggest reasons.

Compare:

"Please donate."

"Your €10 donation helps provide meals for a family in need."

The second gives people a concrete reason to act.

### 3. People don't trust the website enough

Before donating, visitors may wonder:

Is this organization legitimate?

Where does my money go?

Is my payment secure?

Will my donation actually make a difference?

Try adding:

Real photographs.

Testimonials.

Transparent financial information.

Information about who runs the organization.

A secure payment explanation.

### 4. Your donation process is too complicated

Every additional step creates an opportunity for someone to leave.

Ideally:

Make donating possible in just a few clicks.

Offer popular preset amounts.

Allow one-time and recurring donations.

Avoid unnecessary registration.

### 5. Your donation request is too vague

People respond better to specific outcomes.

For example:

Donation

What it achieves

€5

Provides basic supplies

€15

Supports one person

€30

Helps fund a specific project

Only use amounts and outcomes you can genuinely substantiate.

### 6. You're asking too soon

Visitors who have just discovered your website might not be ready to donate.

First, help them understand:

What you do.

Why it matters.

Who benefits.

Why your work deserves support.

Then ask for the donation.

### 7. Your website might not be converting visitors

There is an important distinction between people visiting your website and people being interested in donating.

For example:

1,000 visitors and 2 donations: potentially a conversion problem.

20 visitors and 0 donations: potentially a traffic problem.

You need to understand where people drop off.
