# 16: Is GitHub Sponsors worth it for a small open source project?

**Short answer:** Yes, it's usually worth setting up, as long as you expect little income from it. It costs almost nothing and takes about 30 minutes. It rarely pays meaningfully for a small project, though.

**Why it's worth it**
- **Low cost:** Fee reports I found say GitHub charges no platform fee for Sponsors. Payment processing fees may still apply. Those reports came from third-party sources, not GitHub's own docs, so check GitHub's Sponsors documentation before relying on them.
- **Low effort:** It's built into GitHub. You get a Sponsor button on your repo and a `FUNDING.yml` file, with no separate platform to maintain.
- **Little downside:** You can set it up once and leave it.
- **Credibility:** Sponsors can show up on your profile. Companies that use your project sometimes find it easier to pay through GitHub, because it fits their existing billing.
- **Scale:** GitHub says Sponsors has paid out over $100M to open source. That figure is self-reported, not independently audited.

**Why you shouldn't expect much**
- Money is concentrated among a small number of popular projects and maintainers. A small project often earns $0–$50 a month, and many earn nothing.
- People sponsor maintainers they know or projects they depend on heavily. Stars and downloads don't predict sponsorship well.
- Sponsorship rarely replaces income unless you have a large audience or corporate users.

**What improves your odds**
1. **Say what the money does:** For example, "covers CI costs" or "funds 5 hours a month of maintenance."
2. **Pitch to companies:** Businesses that depend on your project are your best prospects. Mention sponsorship in the README, release notes, and docs.
3. **Offer small perks:** A logo in the README, a name in the release notes, or priority issue triage all help.
4. **Keep it visible but low-key:** Use the Sponsor button and a short README line. Don't nag users.
5. **Add other channels if needed:** Open Collective, Ko-fi, and Polar are common alternatives. Open Collective is better if you want transparent, shared project funds. GitHub Sponsors is better for funding an individual maintainer.

**Caveats**
- Check whether your region is supported and whether you need a Stripe account for payouts.
- Income is taxable, so keep records.
- If you want funding for a team or fiscal transparency, a fiscal host such as Open Collective may suit you better than GitHub Sponsors.

**Bottom line:** Enable it, because it costs little. Treat any income as a bonus, and don't plan around it.
