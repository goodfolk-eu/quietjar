# 17: What's the best way to accept donations on a documentation site?

For a documentation site, the best approach is usually a recurring-donation platform linked from the site, rather than a custom payment form.

**Best options by situation**

| Situation | Recommended option |
|---|---|
| Open-source project | **GitHub Sponsors** (no fees for individuals, and it sits next to your repo) or **Open Collective** (transparent budgets, fiscal hosting) |
| Individual maintainer or small team | **Ko-fi** or **Buy Me a Coffee** (quick setup, one-time and recurring) |
| Want membership tiers or perks | **Patreon** |
| Want full control and the lowest fees | **Stripe Payment Links** (no code, hosted checkout) |
| Registered nonprofit | **Every.org**, **Givebutter**, or **Stripe** with tax-receipt handling |

**Implementation tips**
1. **Use a hosted page, not your own form.** This avoids PCI compliance and security work. Stripe Payment Links and the platforms above handle it for you.
2. **Add a `FUNDING.yml`.** If you're on GitHub, putting it in `.github/` adds a "Sponsor" button to your repo.
3. **Keep placement unobtrusive.** Good spots are a footer link, a sidebar or nav item ("Support this project"), and a short note at the end of key pages. Avoid popups and banners, because they annoy readers and hurt trust.
4. **Say what the money does.** Something like "Funds hosting and maintainer time" converts better than a generic ask.
5. **Offer both one-time and recurring options.** Recurring gives you predictable income, but many readers prefer a single gift.
6. **Show sponsors.** A sponsors page or logo wall gives companies a reason to give.
7. **Mind the fees.** Typical costs are about 2.9% + $0.30 for Stripe, 0% platform fees for GitHub Sponsors (though payment processing still applies for orgs), and 5–10% for Patreon.
8. **Check the legal and tax side.** Donations are usually taxable income unless you're a registered nonprofit. Fiscal hosts like Open Collective can handle this for you.

**Docs framework notes**
- **Docusaurus, MkDocs, VitePress and similar:** add a nav or footer link in the config, or a custom button component.
- **Embeds:** most platforms offer a button or widget, but a plain link is lighter and more accessible.
