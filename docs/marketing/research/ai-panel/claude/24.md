# 24: One-time donations or monthly memberships, which works better for a small creator?

**Short answer: for most small creators, monthly memberships work better as the main model. Keep one-time donations as a low-friction add-on.**

**Why memberships usually win**
- **Predictable income.** Recurring revenue makes it easier to plan and spend time on your work.
- **Higher lifetime value.** A $5/month supporter who stays 8 months gives $40. Most one-time donors give $5–$10 once.
- **Better retention economics.** Keeping a supporter costs less than finding a new one.
- **Room for perks.** Tiers let you offer early access, behind-the-scenes content, or community access.

**Why one-time donations still matter**
- **Lower commitment.** Many people will give $5 once but won't sign up for a subscription.
- **Good for spikes.** Viral posts, launches, and emergencies bring in donations from people who found you that day.
- **No content obligation.** You don't owe a recurring deliverable.
- **Fits some audiences.** If your work is a free tool, a one-off project, or open-source software, a tip jar often fits better than a "membership."

**Where memberships struggle**
- You have to deliver something regularly, which can lead to burnout.
- Churn is real. Expect to lose a noticeable share of members each month.
- With a small audience, a membership with no clear perks can feel awkward to join.

**Practical approach**
1. Offer both. Make monthly the default or the highlighted option, and add a visible "give once" option.
2. Keep tiers simple: one or two, with a low entry price ($3–$5).
3. Offer perks you can sustain, such as a monthly update, a supporter-only post, or credits and thanks.
4. Match the model to your work. Regular output (newsletters, podcasts, videos, serials) suits memberships. Occasional or utility-type work suits tips.
5. Track your numbers. If most of your income comes from one-time gifts after a few months, lean into that.

**Rule of thumb:** If you publish on a schedule and have a core audience, start with memberships. If your output is sporadic or your audience is casual, start with donations and add a membership once you have regular supporters.

Platforms like Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Patreon, and Substack support both. Fee structures differ, so check current rates before choosing.
