# 21: Does a donate button on a website actually make money?

Sometimes, but usually not much. It depends mostly on how much traffic you have and how much your audience feels connected to you.

**What tends to happen**
- **Conversion is low.** Typically well under 1% of visitors donate, and often closer to 0.1% or less on a general website. A site with 10,000 monthly visitors might get a handful of donations a month, often a few dollars each.
- **Most sites earn little.** Personal blogs, small open-source projects, and hobby sites usually get pocket change. That might cover hosting but not much more.
- **A few do well.** Wikipedia, Signal, and some creators, open-source maintainers, and independent journalists raise a lot. They share some traits:
  - a loyal, engaged audience
  - a clear cause or a product people depend on
  - repeated, explicit asks

**What improves results**
- **Explain what the money is for.** "Help cover server costs" or "fund 10 more hours a month of development" works better than a bare "Donate" button.
- **Offer recurring giving.** Monthly supporters bring in much more over time than one-off donors.
- **Place the ask well.** Put it where people feel they got value, such as after a useful article, a download, or a completed task. A footer link is easy to miss.
- **Make it low-friction.** Support Apple Pay and Google Pay, and use preset amounts. Avoid forcing account creation.
- **Show social proof and impact.** Supporter counts, progress toward goals, and thank-you notes help.
- **Build the relationship.** A newsletter, updates, and transparency about how funds are used all help more than the button does.

**Realistic expectations**
- A button on its own is usually passive income of $0–$50 a month for small sites.
- To make meaningful money, you need an audience in the tens of thousands or more, or a smaller audience that is very devoted.
- Memberships, sponsorships, or selling something (a product, a course, a paid tier) often earn more than donations for the same audience.
