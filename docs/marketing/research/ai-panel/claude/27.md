# 27: How can a musician accept tips on their own website?

A musician has several ways to take tips on their own site. I'm answering from general knowledge and haven't searched, so check current fees and features before you commit.

**1. Tip-jar services (easiest)**
- **Stripe Payment Links**: You create a "pay what you want" link in the Stripe dashboard and add it as a button or link on your site. It needs no code, and fees are typically about 2.9% + 30¢ per transaction.
- **PayPal.Me / PayPal Donate button**: Widely recognized, and it takes a few minutes to set up.
- **Ko-fi**: Has a tip button or widget you can embed. It charges no platform fee on one-off tips, though payment processor fees still apply.
- **Buy Me a Coffee**: Similar to Ko-fi, with embeddable buttons and widgets.
- **Venmo / Cash App / Zelle**: Good for casual tips, especially in the US. Link to your profile or show a QR code. Venmo and Cash App have business or creator profile rules, so check their terms.

**2. Build it into your own site**
- **Stripe Checkout** or **Stripe Elements**: You get full control over the look and the amount field, but it needs some development work.
- **Square Online or Square payment links**: A good fit if you also sell merch or tickets with Square.
- **Website builder features**: Squarespace, Wix, Shopify and WordPress (with plugins like GiveWP or WooCommerce) can add a "pay what you want" product or donation form.

**3. Music-specific options**
- **Bandcamp**: Fans can pay more than the asking price on your music. You can link to it from your site.
- **Patreon or Memberships**: Better if you want recurring support instead of one-off tips.

**4. Tips for more tips**
- Put a **QR code** on your site, in your social profiles and on stage banners, so fans can tip from their phones.
- Make the button visible, with clear wording like "Support my music."
- Offer **preset amounts** ($3, $5, $10) plus a custom option.
- Add a short note on where the money goes, such as recording costs or gas for tours.
- Make sure it works well on mobile, since most tips come from phones.

**5. Practical considerations**
- **Fees**: Compare processor fees. Small tips lose a larger share to the fixed 30¢ charge.
- **Taxes**: Tips are generally taxable income, so keep records.
- **Payouts**: Check how fast each service pays out and whether it supports your country.
- **Legal**: Don't call tips "donations" unless you're a registered nonprofit.

**Quick recommendation:** For the least effort, create a Stripe Payment Link or a Ko-fi page and put the button on your site. If you want a custom look, use Stripe Checkout or your website builder's payment feature.
