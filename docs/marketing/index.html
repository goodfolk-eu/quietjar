Boilerplate (about 110 words)

QuietJar is a goal-based support button for people who own their own website: open source developers, writers, musicians, and anyone who earns support from an audience on a site they control. The site owner sets a goal for a real cost. The button shows up, supporters cover it in two taps with Apple Pay, Google Pay, cards, and iDEAL, and the button steps aside until the next goal, so the ask never turns into wallpaper. It is engineered like a payments SDK: 11.7 kB gzipped, about 40 ms to load, no layout shift. After someone gives, the button steps back instead of nagging. There is no monthly fee; QuietJar takes 5% of what you receive and covers card fees. QuietJar is open source under the AGPL-3.0 license and built in the EU, on European rails and hosting.

FAQ answers

Answers, ready to paste

The answers that come up most in support and community threads. These match the site FAQ word for word.

What is QuietJar?

A small, polished support button for your own website. Give it a goal for a real cost, and it only asks until that cost is covered.

What does it cost?

No monthly fee. No plans to compare. QuietJar takes 5% of each donation, minimum €0.50, and pays the card fees. Donations are €1 to €500. It costs you nothing until it is earning you something.

When do I get paid?

Donations reach your QuietJar balance after a few days. Once your available balance reaches 50 EUR (or the local equivalent), pay it out to your bank account from your dashboard with one click.

Will it slow down my site?

No. The script is 11.7 kB gzipped, loads in about 40 ms and causes no layout shift.

How is this different from the tools I know?

Most tools have earned their place. Ko-fi charges 5% on tips by default (0% only on a paid Gold plan) and Buy Me a Coffee has the friendliest brand around. Both have goals, but their button keeps asking whether the goal is met or not. QuietJar asks while there is a real cost to cover, then goes quiet.

Will my visitors trust paying on my site?

They pay through the same regulated rails as any checkout. What they see is Apple Pay, Google Pay, or their own bank's iDEAL screen. Nothing unfamiliar, nothing to type.

Logo usage rules (plain text)

Ink mark and lockup: on light backgrounds (paper #faf9f6, white). White mark and lockup: on dark backgrounds (field #14201c, footer #0e1714). Blue-green lockup: two-color partner and print contexts on light backgrounds. Clear space: keep at least the height of the mark free on all sides. Minimum sizes: mark 16 px, lockup 120 px wide. Never stretch, recolor, or place the ink logo on a busy background.

Voice rules

How we talk

Quiet confidence, not hype. Plain language, short lines, lead with goals that end. When adapting any copy above, keep these rules.

Voice rules (plain text)

Lead with goals that end: ask when there is a real cost, then go quiet. Never claim Ko-fi or Buy Me a Coffee redirect; both have on-page widgets. Plain benefit-led language for a non-technical reader. Short lines, breathing room, sentence case headings. No exclamation marks, no hype words, no charity framing. Say: support, supporters, button, steps back, goals. Not: tip, donate (outside search-facing titles), donors, fans, users, suppression, campaigns. Never: coffee, cups, beans, "buy me a", revolutionary, seamless, game-changing, empower, supercharge. QuietJar, one word, capital Q and J in prose; lowercase quietjar in the wordmark and domain. A senior engineer who is also a good host: competent, calm, a little dry, warm when it matters.
