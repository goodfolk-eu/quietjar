If you are not a registered charity, your shortlist is shorter than most "best donation widget" lists suggest. Givebutter, Zeffy and Give Lively only accept organizations. For individuals, creators and small projects, the realistic options are QuietJar, Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Donorbox and a form builder like Jotform.

Which one fits depends on what you want the widget to do. Below we compare them on who can sign up, how supporters pay, what it costs, and how it sits on your page.

## Why do most widget lists not apply to you?

Because most of them were written for nonprofits. Ask an AI assistant for an embeddable donation widget and you get Donorbox, Givebutter, Give Lively and similar tools. They are good products, built for registered charities with tax receipts, donor databases and US bank accounts.

If you run a blog, a free tool, an open source project or a podcast, many of them will not let you in. We checked each tool's own pricing page and help center, so you do not find out halfway through sign-up.

## What should you compare?

Seven things decide whether a widget works for a small site:

- **Who can sign up.** Individual, or registered nonprofit only.
- **Wallets.** Apple Pay and Google Pay turn a donation into two taps on a phone.
- **Local methods.** In the Netherlands, many people expect iDEAL. In Belgium, Bancontact.
- **No donor account.** Every extra form field loses people.
- **Design fit.** Does it look like part of your site, or like someone else's brand?
- **Layout shift and load.** A widget that pushes your content down or slows the page costs you readers.
- **Fees.** The platform fee, plus the payment processing that often comes on top.

## The options for individuals, one by one

### Ko-fi

Ko-fi is the cheapest option on paper. One-off tips carry no Ko-fi fee if you switch off its Standard mode, and the money goes straight into your own Stripe or PayPal account. New creators start with Standard mode on, which charges 5% on tips in exchange for extra features, so check your settings.

Its website widget comes as a floating button or a tip panel, and supporters pay without leaving your site or creating an account. With Stripe connected, Ko-fi shows Apple Pay, Google Pay and local bank methods in several European countries. It also has memberships, a shop and commissions.

**Best for:** creators who want a hosted page, a shop or memberships next to their tips, and who want the lowest platform fee.

The question is what the button does all month. It asks every visitor, every day, whether you need the money that month or not.

### Buy Me a Coffee

Buy Me a Coffee has the friendliest brand in this space and a huge installed base on blogs and READMEs. Its floating widget takes payment on your page, supporters do not need an account, and its FAQ lists cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Cash App.

It charges 5% per transaction with no monthly fee. Stripe processing comes on top: 2.9% + $0.30, plus 1% on transactions outside the US, according to its help center.

**Best for:** creators who like the coffee metaphor and want memberships or a shop on the same account.

We did not find iDEAL or Bancontact. And the coffee cup is part of the deal. Does it fit the rest of your site?

### Donorbox

Donorbox is the strongest of the nonprofit tools that also accepts individuals. Its FAQ says nonprofits, for-profit organizations and individual fundraisers can create donation pages. You get embedded forms, popup forms and a sticky donate button.

Donorbox lists cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, SEPA, iDEAL and PayPal for European donors. The free Standard plan charges a 2.95% platform fee on donation forms, with Stripe or PayPal fees on top. Pro costs $150 a month.

**Best for:** a small organization or club that wants donor management, recurring giving and reports.

For a personal site, it is a lot of machinery. The forms are built for campaigns, not for a quiet €3 thank-you.

### Jotform

Jotform is a form builder with a donation template. It does not take a transaction fee, and with its Stripe Checkout integration supporters can pay with Apple Pay, Google Pay, iDEAL and more.

The catch is the plan limits. The free Starter plan allows 10 payment submissions a month. Bronze costs $39 a month for 100.

**Best for:** people who already use Jotform for other forms and get a handful of donations a month.

### QuietJar

QuietJar is a small donation widget for your own website. You sign up as yourself, no nonprofit status needed. Supporters pay right on your page with Apple Pay, Google Pay, cards, Link or iDEAL, and where available Bancontact, EPS, BLIK and others. Local methods need a short request in the dashboard first, and cards work while you wait.

What makes it different is the goal. You set a goal for a real cost, like this month's hosting, and the button shows progress. When supporters cover it, the button hides itself until the next month. We wrote more about [why a button that disappears works](/articles/donate-button-that-disappears-when-goal-is-met/). The widget is [open source under the AGPL-3.0](https://codefloe.com/Goodfolk.eu/quietjar), so you can check what it adds to your page before you add it.

**Best for:** creators, writers and developers who want support to happen on their own site, tied to a real cost.

QuietJar has no memberships, no shop and no WordPress plugin. If you need those, Ko-fi or Buy Me a Coffee is the better choice.

### Not for individuals: Givebutter, Zeffy and Give Lively

These three are excellent if you qualify, and you probably do not.

Givebutter's help center says "individual (personal) fundraising accounts are not supported", and organizations outside the US need a US bank account. Zeffy is free for nonprofits, but only registered ones in the US, Canada, the UK, Ireland, Australia and Germany. Give Lively is for US 501(c)(3) organizations.

**Best for:** registered nonprofits. If that is you, Zeffy's free model is hard to beat.

## How does the widget affect your page?

This is the part most comparisons skip. A donation widget is third-party code on every page of your site, so it should cost your readers as little as possible.

The QuietJar script is 38.6 kB, or 11.7 kB gzipped, and loads in around 40 ms on a cold cache. It floats in a fixed corner layer, so it causes zero layout shift. Stripe's payment code only loads when someone opens the checkout, and the widget sets no cookies, so you need no cookie banner for it.

We did not measure the other widgets, so we will not quote numbers for them. You can check any widget yourself: run [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) on your page with and without it, and compare the layout shift score and the total blocking time.

## What do the fees mean for a small donation?

Platform fees look similar until you add processing. Most tools charge a platform fee, and then Stripe or PayPal takes its own cut on top.

QuietJar works the other way. It charges 5% per donation, minimum €0.50, and pays the card and payment fees itself. On a €10 donation you receive €9.50, whether the supporter paid with Apple Pay, an American Express card or iDEAL. There is no monthly fee. See [pricing](/pricing/) for details.

Ko-fi with Standard mode off is cheaper on paper for one-off tips, because you only pay processing. If low fees matter more to you than the checkout or the goal, that is a fair reason to pick it.

## So which one should you choose?

Pick based on what you need the widget to do:

- **Memberships, a shop or a hosted creator page:** Ko-fi or Buy Me a Coffee.
- **Donor management for a small organization:** Donorbox.
- **A registered nonprofit in the US, Canada or the UK:** Zeffy or Givebutter.
- **A few donations a month on a form you already have:** Jotform.
- **A quiet, fast widget on your own site, tied to a real cost:** QuietJar.
