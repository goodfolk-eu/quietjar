Ko-fi is the best Buy Me a Coffee alternative for most creators. One-off tips can carry no platform fee, the money lands straight in your own PayPal or Stripe account, and its widget takes payment right on your page. If you own a website and want support to happen there, tied to a real cost, QuietJar is the option built for that.

The rest depends on what you ask for. GitHub Sponsors suits maintainers, Patreon suits memberships, Liberapay suits recurring donations with no platform cut, and Stripe Payment Links suit people who want to build it themselves. Every fee below was checked on the platform's own site in November 2026.

## What are we comparing, exactly?

This list is for people who own a website: a blog, a docs site, a web app, a portfolio. That changes the question. A profile page you share on social media is one thing. A button that lives on your own site, in front of your own readers, is another.

So for each option we looked at four things. What it costs, in its own currency. Whether supporters can pay without leaving your page. Which payment methods they get. And what the button does once you have what you need.

## Why do people leave Buy Me a Coffee?

Buy Me a Coffee has the friendliest brand in the space and a floating widget that takes payment on your site. People leave for two reasons: the fees, and the coffee cup.

Its help center lists a 5% platform fee per transaction, with Stripe's fees on top: 2.9% + $0.30, then 0.5% for payouts, and 1% more for transactions from outside the US. On a small tip, the $0.30 does the most damage. On a $5 coffee it is 6% on its own.

The coffee metaphor is the other reason. It fits a blog. Does it fit a developer tool, a podcast, or a community site that needs its server bill paid?

## 1. Ko-fi: the best all-round alternative

Ko-fi is the answer most creators land on, and it earns that spot. In Ko-fi Free mode, one-off tips carry no service fee at all. Everything else, like memberships, monthly tips, commissions and shop sales, costs 5%. Payments go straight into your own PayPal or Stripe account, so there is no waiting for a payout.

For your website, Ko-fi offers a floating button and a tip panel that let supporters tip or join a membership without leaving the page. On Ko-fi, connecting Stripe adds cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay.

Two things to watch. New creators start in Standard mode, which charges 5% on one-off tips as well. You can switch it off under **Settings > Payment**, which answers the common "does Ko-fi charge 0% or 5%?" question: it depends on that switch. Processing fees from PayPal or Stripe come on top, which Ko-fi puts at "typically around 3% + $0.30". Ko-fi Gold still exists for new users at $12 a month, monthly only.

Ko-fi's goals live on your Ko-fi page, and the button on your site keeps asking whether the goal is met or not. **Best for:** creators who want tips, a shop and memberships in one place, at the lowest platform fee. See [QuietJar vs Ko-fi](/compare/ko-fi/) for a side-by-side.

## 2. QuietJar: best for support on your own website

QuietJar is a donation widget, not a profile page. You set a goal for a real cost, like this month's hosting, and the button shows progress as supporters chip in. When the goal is met, the button steps aside until the next month or the next goal.

That is the one thing no other tool on this list does. TCG Pocket Collection Tracker set a €50 goal for its September 2026 hosting and raised €97 in 12 hours. The same site's Buy Me a Coffee button had averaged about €30 a month.

Supporters pay on your page with Apple Pay, Google Pay, cards, Link and iDEAL, and where available Bancontact, EPS, Revolut Pay, BLIK, Satispay and Pay by Bank. After someone gives, the widget stays hidden for them for 30 days by default and thanks them instead of asking again. The script loads in about 40 ms, causes no layout shift, and sets no cookies. It is also [open source](https://codefloe.com/Goodfolk.eu/quietjar), so you can read every line that runs on your page.

The price is [5% per donation, minimum €0.50](/pricing/), and QuietJar pays the card fees out of that. There is no monthly fee. That minimum matters for tiny donations: on €5 QuietJar takes €0.50, a little more than the roughly $0.45 a $5 Ko-fi tip loses to processing. At €10, QuietJar's 5% all-in is less than Ko-fi or Buy Me a Coffee take once you count their processing.

QuietJar has no memberships, no shop and no exclusive posts. **Best for:** website owners who want occasional, specific support on their own site, especially with a European audience.

## 3. Liberapay: best for recurring donations with no platform cut

Liberapay is a non-profit based in France. It takes no cut of donations and funds itself through donations to its own account. You still pay processing: Liberapay reports an average of about 3% through Stripe and 5.1% through PayPal over the past year.

It is built for recurring donations. One-time donations "aren't properly supported yet", in its own words. Your website links to your Liberapay page, and giving happens there.

**Best for:** open source and non-commercial projects with supporters who will give every month. For more European options, see [a European alternative to Buy Me a Coffee and Patreon](/articles/european-alternative-to-buy-me-a-coffee-and-patreon/).

## 4. GitHub Sponsors: best for open source maintainers

GitHub Sponsors charges no fees on sponsorships from personal accounts. Sponsorships from organizations cost up to 6%. Sponsors can choose one-time or monthly amounts, and you decide which tiers to offer. A `FUNDING.yml` file puts a Sponsor button on your repository.

The gap is reach. Sponsors pay through GitHub, and the button lives on GitHub. How many of your users ever open the repo, compared to how many read your docs?

**Best for:** maintainers whose supporters are developers with GitHub accounts. If most of your users never see the repo, read [how to add a support button to your docs site](/articles/add-a-support-button-to-your-docs-site/).

## 5. Patreon: best for memberships

Patreon owns the "support a creator every month" idea. New creators pay 10% of their income, plus payment processing, currency conversion and payout fees. It supports more than 16 currencies.

In return you get a full membership platform: tiers, exclusive posts, a community. QuietJar does not do memberships, and neither does a simple tip button. If your supporters expect something back every month, Patreon is the stronger choice.

**Best for:** creators with a regular output that members pay to access. It is not built to sit on your website as a quick, one-off ask.

## 6. Stripe Payment Links: best if you want to build it yourself

Stripe Payment Links let you create a payment page without code, including one where the customer chooses the amount. Stripe lists "accept a donation" as a use case. You can share the link or embed a buy button on your site. The checkout itself opens on a Stripe-hosted page.

There is no platform fee, only Stripe's processing. In Stripe's Irish pricing, that is 1.5% + €0.25 for standard European cards, 2.5% + €0.25 for UK cards, and 3.15% + €0.25 for international cards. That is the lowest price on this list for a one-off card payment.

What you do not get is everything around it: a goal, a progress bar, a button that hides when the goal is met, or one that leaves recent supporters alone. You can build those, with webhooks, a running total and a monthly reset. The question is how many weekends that takes, and who fixes it when it breaks. Read more in [can you use Stripe to accept donations](/articles/can-you-use-stripe-to-accept-donations/).

**Best for:** developers who want the lowest fee and do not mind the upkeep.

## What does a €10 donation leave you?

A rough example, using each platform's published rates for a 10-unit tip in its own currency. Real numbers move with the supporter's card, country and currency.

| | Supporter gives | Fees | You keep, roughly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ko-fi, Free mode | $10 | About $0.60 processing | $9.40 |
| Ko-fi, Standard mode | $10 | $0.50 service fee + about $0.60 processing | $8.90 |
| Buy Me a Coffee | $10 | $0.50 + $0.59 processing, before payout fee | $8.91 |
| QuietJar | €10 | €0.50, card fees included | €9.50 |
| Stripe Payment Link | €10 | €0.40 on a standard EEA card | €9.60 |

Stripe on its own is cheaper per tip than QuietJar, and so is Ko-fi Free on larger tips, where its fixed $0.30 matters less. GitHub Sponsors and Liberapay can be cheaper too. QuietJar's fee pays for what sits around the payment: the goal, the wallets and local methods, and a button that knows when to stop asking.

## Which alternative should you choose?

Match the tool to the ask. One clear ask beats three buttons in a row.

- **You want tips, a shop and memberships on a profile page:** Ko-fi.
- **You want supporters to cover a real cost on your own site:** QuietJar. See [how to add a donation goal progress bar to your website](/articles/how-to-add-a-donation-goal-progress-bar-to-your-website/).
- **You maintain open source:** GitHub Sponsors in the repo, plus a goal on your docs site if your users never open GitHub.
- **You want paying members:** Patreon, or Steady if you prefer a European company.
- **You want monthly donations with no platform cut:** Liberapay.
- **You are a developer with spare weekends:** Stripe Payment Links.

For a wider look that includes nonprofit tools, see [the best donation widgets for your website](/articles/best-donation-widgets-for-your-website/). For a straight comparison with the tool you are leaving, see [QuietJar vs Buy Me a Coffee](/compare/buymeacoffee/).

Ko-fi is the safe, popular choice, and in Free mode it is very cheap for one-off tips. If your site has a real cost to cover and you would rather ask only while it is open, [add QuietJar to your site](/). It takes a few minutes, with no monthly fee and [5% per donation](/pricing/).
