Yes, there are good European alternatives. Liberapay and Tipeee are French, Steady is German, Petje.af is Dutch, and we build QuietJar in Amsterdam. Which one fits depends on one question: do you want to sell memberships, or do you want people to support you on your own website?

For memberships with perks and exclusive posts, Steady is the closest European match to Patreon. For simple support without a profile page in between, QuietJar puts a donation widget on your own site. The details are below, checked on each platform's own site in November 2026.

## What does "European" actually mean for a donation platform?

It means four different things, and a platform can tick some boxes and miss others. Before you switch, check each one.

- **Where the company is.** This decides which laws apply to it and where you go if something goes wrong.
- **Where the data lives.** A European company can still host your supporters' data outside Europe.
- **Which payment methods work.** Your supporters in the Netherlands want iDEAL, in Belgium Bancontact, in Germany often SEPA Direct Debit or PayPal. Cards alone lose people.
- **Who can receive payouts.** Some platforms only pay out to creators in certain countries.

Most platforms on this list use a payment company to move the money. Liberapay uses Stripe and PayPal, Petje.af uses Mollie or Stripe, and QuietJar is built on Stripe. So a European company does not mean every part of the chain is European. It does mean you deal with a company under EU rules, in a language and time zone close to yours.

## Why do people look for something other than Buy Me a Coffee and Patreon?

Both are good products with a huge reach, and that is why they are the default. But Europeans run into the same few things.

Buy Me a Coffee takes 5% per transaction, and its help center lists Stripe's processing on top: 2.9% + $0.30, a 0.5% payout fee, and 1% extra for transactions from outside the US. Patreon charges new creators 10% of their income, plus processing, currency conversion and payout fees. If your supporters pay in euros and you get paid in euros, it is fair to ask why your money takes a detour through dollar-priced fees.

The other reason is payment methods. A Dutch reader who wants to give €5 reaches for iDEAL, not a credit card.

## The European options, one by one

### Liberapay: recurring donations with no platform fee

Liberapay is a French non-profit association that started in 2015. It takes no cut of donations and funds itself through donations to its own account. You still pay processing fees: Liberapay reports an average of about 3% through Stripe and 5.1% through PayPal over the past year. Its legal page says the site runs on AWS in Ireland.

It is built for recurring donations. One-time donations "aren't properly supported yet", in Liberapay's own words, and there are no membership tiers. European donors can pay by card, PayPal, or SEPA Direct Debit for euro donations. Creators in 33 territories get the full feature set: 32 in Europe, plus the United States.

**Best for:** open source maintainers and non-commercial projects that want steady monthly support with no platform cut. Your website links to your Liberapay page, and giving happens there.

### Tipeee: tips and rewards, in French and English

Tipeee is a French platform for web creators: video makers, writers, podcasters, illustrators. It charges 8% including VAT on what you collect each month. Supporters pay a transaction fee on top of their tip, which shrinks as the amount grows. If part of your money goes to PayPal, PayPal takes 1% of that payout, capped at €6.

Supporters can give one-off or recurring tips, by card or PayPal, and you can offer rewards to recurring supporters. Tipeee works only in euros and is open to creators who live in 31 European countries, including the UK, Switzerland and Norway. It also has an embeddable payment module for your own site, as a pop-in window or an iframe.

**Best for:** French-speaking creators and anyone who wants a Patreon-like page with both one-off and monthly tips.

### Steady: memberships for independent media

Steady was founded in Berlin in 2016 and describes itself as "made for Europe's media makers". It takes 10%, plus the fees of the payment provider. Members can pay with PayPal, Apple Pay, cards, and SEPA Direct Debit for euro memberships.

Steady is a full membership platform, which none of the tip tools on this page are. Monthly or annual plans, exclusive content, paid newsletters, podcasts. You can run it as a standalone page or add it to your own website. One-time donations are not a native feature, though.

**Best for:** magazines, newsletters and podcasts that want paying members, not tips.

### Petje.af: the Dutch Patreon

Petje.af is made by Petje.af B.V. in Wageningen, in the Netherlands. It charges 6% per transaction, plus the transaction fees of Mollie or Stripe. You connect your own Mollie or Stripe account, so the payments and supporter details stay yours.

It offers subscriptions, one-off donations, rewards and exclusive podcasts. With Mollie connected, iDEAL and SEPA Direct Debit are switched on automatically. The site and help pages are in Dutch.

**Best for:** Dutch podcasters and creators with a Dutch-speaking audience who want memberships.

### QuietJar: a donation widget for your own website

QuietJar is made by Goodfolk B.V. in Amsterdam, and its code is [open source under the AGPL-3.0](https://codefloe.com/Goodfolk.eu/quietjar). It is not a profile page with a button attached. It is a small widget that sits on your own website, where supporters pay in a couple of taps without leaving the page.

Supporters can pay with Apple Pay, Google Pay, cards, Link and iDEAL, and where available Bancontact, EPS, Revolut Pay, BLIK, Satispay and Pay by Bank. Payments and data stay on European infrastructure, and the widget sets no cookies, so you need no cookie banner for it. Creators in 49 countries can receive payouts, including most of the EU, the UK, Switzerland and Norway.

The price is [5% per donation, minimum €0.50](/pricing/), and QuietJar pays the card fees out of that. What makes it different is the goal: you set one for a real cost, like this month's hosting, and the button steps aside once it is covered. TCG Pocket Collection Tracker raised €97 in 12 hours on a €50 goal for its September 2026 hosting, against about €30 a month from its old Buy Me a Coffee button.

**Best for:** people who own a website and want occasional, specific support there. No website? Every account also gets a hosted page at `quietjar.com/@yourname`.

QuietJar has no memberships, no exclusive posts and no shop. If that is what you need, Steady or Patreon is the better choice, and we would rather tell you that now.

## Which one should you pick?

Start from what your supporters get in return, then pick the tool that fits.

- **They get exclusive content or perks:** Steady, or Petje.af for a Dutch audience. Tipeee if you want one-off tips next to memberships.
- **They support your work with no perks, every month:** Liberapay, especially for open source.
- **They help cover a real cost on your website:** QuietJar. Set a goal, let the button show progress, and let it step aside when the cost is covered.

You can also combine them. A newsletter on Steady and a QuietJar goal for your hosting bill do different jobs. Just keep the ask clear, because two buttons side by side force people to choose, and that can mean they pick neither.

## Can your supporters pay the way they are used to?

This is the part most comparison lists skip, and the part your supporters notice first. A checkout that only takes cards and PayPal asks a Dutch or Belgian supporter to dig out a card they may not use online.

QuietJar has iDEAL built in, and Bancontact, EPS, BLIK and others where your account's country supports them. Local methods need a short request in the dashboard and a review, and cards work in the meantime. If you want memberships, Steady is the strongest European choice, with Petje.af and Tipeee close behind for Dutch and French audiences.
